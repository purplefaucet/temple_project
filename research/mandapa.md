# Front halls (mandapa): research notes for the build

Notes for adding the halls in front of the tower, written so a build session can work from them without the source books. Summaries and citations only. Quotations are kept short. Compiled 18 October 2026.

Scope reminder: the project brief says "exterior architecture only", with no sanctum interior and no deity imagery. **Decide before building:** showing the 6 × 6 pillar grid inside the great hall, or walking into the halls, goes beyond "exterior only". Either keep the halls as closed exteriors, or widen the scope to hall interiors and stop at the sanctum door. Guardian figures and the Nandi bull are figure sculpture, so they are omitted or shown as blank "Invented" blocks.

## 2. Names and sequence: reconciling the sources

Order from the east entrance westward to the sanctum, at Thanjavur:

1. **Nandi pavilion.** Free-standing, on the main axis ([BS-T, Mahamandapa](https://www.wisdomlib.org/south-asia/book/middle-chola-temples/d/doc210434.html)). WP dates the Nandi shrine to the Nayaka period (16th–17th c.), citing Michell 2008. BS-T gives the monolithic bull as 3.65 m high, 5.94 m long and 2.59 m broad, and no date for the pavilion was found in the excerpts read. **Not part of the hall block. Treat as a later phase.**
2. **Mukhamandapa** (front hall or porch). Reached by "a broad, sweeping flight of steps" (BS-T).
3. **Mahamandapa** (great hall). At Thanjavur it has "rows of six pillars both east to west and north to south" (BS-T), so a 6 × 6 grid of 36 pillars. WP, citing Balasubrahmanyam 1975, calls the mukha- and mahamandapa "square plan structures".
4. **Ardhamandapa** (the hall directly in front of the sanctum). It has **side gateways on the north and south**, each reached by a two-stage stair: courtyard to the top of the sub-base, a landing, then a second flight to the threshold, with "massive sinuous and ornamental balustrades" and large guardian figures (BS-T, Ardhamandapa). It held the deity's bathing platform.
5. **Garbhagriha** (sanctum, inside the tower). **Stop here.**

**Reconciliation (about 75% confident).** WP's five sections are Nandi hall, mukhamandapa, mahamandapa, "antrala" and sanctum. WP's "antrala, the pavilion that connects the great hall with the sanctum", is the same space BS-T calls the **ardhamandapa**: the transverse hall with the north and south gateways. Sahapedia's "small chamber with doorways leading to huge staircases on each side" fits the same space. So there are **three halls** between the Nandi and the sanctum: mukha-, maha- and ardhamandapa. The disagreement is over names, not over the number of halls.

**Text versus building on the "covered passage".** The Mayamata's *antarala* (MM 19.6–7) is a short passage only about two modules long. A module is the width of the top of a pillar (MM 15.28b–29a). The great Chola temples instead have a full hall with side entrances in that position (the ardhamandapa, about 20 m long at Gangaikondacholapuram; see section 4). Model the building, and note the text's smaller version.

## 3. What the texts prescribe

### Mayamata (read directly)

- **Width of the hall in front of the shrine:** equal to the shrine's width, or ¾ or ½ of it (MM 19.4b–5; repeated 21.18b). If there is a covered passage and a porch, the hall is the full width of the temple (MM 19.5).
- **Pillars:** an even number, arranged symmetrically (MM 19.5).
- **Elevation:** the hall "comprises all the prescribed elements". Dagens glosses this as three levels, base, level of pillars and entablature, similar to the temple's own (MM 19.5 n. 7, citing 25.25 and 25.188).
- **Top of the hall:** either decorated like the shrine, with corner and elongated pavilions on its entablature, or with only the three levels and arcatures on the walls (MM 21.19–20 and n. 16).
- **Alignment:** the hall is "on the same line" (*samasutra*) as the shrine, and a covered passage, if present, is on a lower level (MM 21.20 and n. 17).
- **Covered passage (antarala):** 1 or 2 cubits wide or proportional to the temple, and 2 modules long, measured as a projection from the temple's reference line (MM 19.6–7 and n. 8).
- **Porch:** 2 or 3 cubits wide, with a stair at the side whose handrail is shaped like an elephant's trunk (MM 19.6–7). A porch projecting 2 or 3 modules is set on the side and on the front of the hall (MM 19.8).
- **Wall thickness** of the hall and passage: equal to the shrine's wall, or ¾ or ½ of it (MM 19.8).
- **Use:** Dagens calls the mukhamandapa "a kind of nave" where rites are held that the cramped sanctum cannot accommodate (MM 19 n. 6).
- **Unit:** the module is the width of the top of a pillar, and all measurements of a building follow from it (MM 15.28b–29a).
- **From the vol. 1 introduction (Dagens's summary of ch. 25, which is not read)** (MM intro pp. lxxii–lxxiii):
  - The pavilion is usually a three-level elevation of base, pillars and entablature (25.25). Simpler versions have no base (25.26–29).
  - Its unit of proportion is the intercolumniation, real or notional (25.13–16).
  - The roof is usually flat, but may carry raised lanterns over bays where the intercolumniation is doubled or tripled.
  - Pillars are stone or wood.
  - There are notes on the mukhamandapa and on galleries (25.188–97).
  - Halls (*sabha*, 25.198–237) are hypostyle with a large pillar-free centre and a wagon roof.
- **Things in front of the temple** (MM ch. 23), for a later compound phase:
  - Two altars, each half the sanctum's width high and wide (23.74–76).
  - The flagstaff stands in front of the bull, halfway from the temple (23.82–83a).
  - Other buildings are placed in the enclosures, for example the pavilion for ablutions and the pavilion for flowers in the south (23.72b–73, 83b–88).

### Manasara (second witness, via excerpts)
- **Elevation:** pavilions have "three-fold members", base, pillar and entablature (MS 34.63), with a pinnacle and corner towers allowed above the entablature (34.64).
- **Ornament:** entablatures are to be ornamented like the main building (34.70–72).
- **Symmetry:** a pavilion not symmetrical to the main building is inauspicious (34.69).
- **Placement:** many pavilions may stand in front of temples (34.33), and a sequence of seven is listed (34.45–50).
- **Pillar counts:** these vary by type (for example 16 at 34.75, 64 at 34.84, 192 at 34.166).
- **Height:** equal to the breadth or greater (34.7).
- **Not given:** no ratio to the shrine and no rule that halls must be lower.

## 4. Measured data

**Gangaikondacholapuram** (BS-G, quoted verbatim). This is the best plan data available.
- "The upapitham is 103.63 ms (340') long and 30.48 ms (100') wide." This single sub-base runs under the sanctum, the ardhamandapa and the mahamandapa.
- Sanctum and mahamandapa are "respectively 30.48 ms (100') and 53.34 ms (175') long". "The garbhagriha and mahamandapa are also of the same width."
- The ardhamandapa "is shaped like a constricted square neck between the two, of side 19.81 ms (65')". (The OCR'd sentence drops the noun. The identification is supported by the arithmetic 30.48 + 19.81 + 53.34 = 103.63.)
- Ardhamandapa: reached from north and south by two-stage stairs, like Thanjavur's, and "supported by plain, square and heavy pillars".
- **Caution:** at the mahamandapa "only the plinth and remnants of its walls at the western extremity are original, the side walls, pillars and roof having been reconstructed". The original "must have been an imposing structure of twice the present height".

Normalised to the tower's base width W (W = 30.48 m here):

| Part | Length (E–W) | Width (N–S) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sanctum (tower base) | 1.00 W | 1.00 W | BS-G |
| Ardhamandapa | 0.65 W | not given ("constricted", so narrower than W) | BS-G |
| Mahamandapa | 1.75 W | 1.00 W | BS-G |
| Shared sub-base | 3.40 W | 1.00 W | BS-G |

**Thanjavur** (BS-T, via excerpts):
- Mahamandapa: 6 × 6 pillars.
- Halls described as square in plan (WP, citing BS-T).
- The sanctum and ardhamandapa floors are "almost about 6.1 ms" above the outer courtyard because the base is so high.
- No hall lengths, widths or heights were found in the sources read.
- The tower's base is 30.18 m square, with a 1.40 m sub-base and a 3.60 m moulded base (already in the model).
- The 6.1 m floor height against the 5.0 m sub-base plus base is unreconciled. It may include a raised court or the stair landing. Below 80% confident.

## 6. Conflicts and choices to flag on the page

- **Text versus building on the passage:** the Mayamata's two-module covered passage against the Chola ardhamandapa hall about 20 m long. The model should follow the building.
- **Plan of the mahamandapa:** long at GKC (1.75 W) and "square" at Thanjavur (via WP). Thanjavur's own hall dimensions were not found.
- **Reconstruction:** GKC's mahamandapa walls, pillars and roof are rebuilt, and the original was twice the present height. The originality of Thanjavur's halls is not established in the sources read.
- **Hall height:** no source read gives a ratio between hall height and tower height. Keep it an estimate.
- **Names:** WP's "antrala" equals BS-T's ardhamandapa (about 75%).
- **Floor height:** Thanjavur's 6.1 m floor height against the 5.0 m measured sub-base plus base.

- **Pichard, *Tanjavur Brhadisvara: An Architectural Study* (1995).** The likely source for measured plans of Thanjavur's halls.
- **Thanjavur hall widths, lengths, heights and pillar spacing.** None found online in this pass.
