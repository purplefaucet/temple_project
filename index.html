    Front halls

    Three halls in front of the tower on one shared base, as at Gangaikondacholapuram and Thanjavur . In walk mode you can climb the stairs and go in, as far as the closed sanctum door.
    Thanjavur's halls, measured from map outlines, end 67.2 m from the tower's centre with a porch in front . At Gangaikondacholapuram the great hall is 1.75 times as long as the sanctum is wide , but its walls, pillars and roof were rebuilt. Thanjavur's is also called square in plan .
    The Mayamata makes the hall in front of the shrine as wide as it, or three quarters or half of it (19.4b to 5) . In the mapped plan the front hall is the measured porch, so this control is off.

    The court

    The cloistered court of Thanjavur, 241 by 121 m outside, as measured from map outlines and given by the Archaeological Survey of India . With its two Chola gateways on the east , the Chandesvara shrine and the cloister shrines.
    Later additions are the Nayak Nandi pavilion and five later shrines and halls, placed where map outlines show them . Their forms are not described in the sources read and are drawn as plain massing.
    Balasubrahmanyam gives eight shrines for attendant deities and eight for the guardians of the directions in the original plan . Sahapedia counts 36, some now lost .

    Idealized composite, not a model of any specific temple. No sanctum interior and no deity imagery. In walk mode the front halls can be entered, and the walk stops at the closed sanctum door. Figure sculpture in the court, such as the Nandis on the wall and the Nandi bull, is drawn as blank blocks. Dashed buttons are not built yet.

    Walk puts the camera at eye height, 1.6 m, taking the base as 30.18 m wide, the Thanjavur figure the model's base heights are scaled from . The paving pattern is there to show movement and has no source. Up close, the mouldings and pavilions are plain blocks.

      Not verified
      
        
        
          
      • Texts and buildings are different kinds of evidence. Adam Hardy, who has tested texts by drawing them, says of text and measured building: “Often, they do correspond and often, they don't” . A rule taken from a text shows a design tradition. It does not show what Chola builders did.
        • 
          
      • Thanjavur itself breaks the text's rules. The Mayamata describes temples of up to twelve storeys and makes a large one ten sevenths as high as it is wide. Thanjavur has fifteen storeys by Dagens's count and is 2.10 times as high as its base. Dagens calls it “abnormal” against the treatise, and reads a passing mention of thirteen to sixteen storeys as a later addition  . On overall height this model follows the building, not the text.
        • 
          
      • Only the sub-base has every dimension sourced. Switch “Colour by” to Dimensions to see which sizes rest on a cited ratio or measurement and which are estimates.
        • 
          
      • The measurements come from two temples only, both royal foundations. The base heights are Thanjavur's  and the wall height is Gangaikondacholapuram's . Mixing them into one tower is a choice made for this model.
        • 
          
      • The taper in equal steps comes from the Mayamata's three and four-storey temples. The text gives no storey widths above five storeys, so thirteen tiers is an extrapolation .
        • 
          
      • The crown is larger than the Mayamata's ratios would make it. In the text the neck is under half the pillar height of the top storey. Applied to thirteen thin tiers that would give a very flat dome, so the crown's scale here is a choice .
        • 
          
      • How many pavilions a tier carries is set by how many parts fit its side. The Mayamata says pavilions shrink storey by storey and may be arranged on each storey in the same way (22.76 to 78) , and the Manasara gives every storey the same row  . Thinning the rows on the narrower upper tiers is an inference.
        • 
          
      • The Mayamata was read directly in Bruno Dagens's edition: the introduction, chapters 11, 13 and 19 to 22, and parts of 14 and 18. Chapters 15 to 17 and 23 are unread. Volume 2, which holds the chapter on gateways, has not been consulted, and neither has any Agama. Verse numbers are given only where they could be read in the scan.
        • 
          
      • Dagens places the Mayamata between the early 9th and the late 12th century, and thinks it earlier than the great temples of the early 11th. The foreword to his edition says 11th to 12th century . Hardy's “before the end of the tenth century”  sits at the early end of that range.
        • 
          
      • The Manasara's date is contested. P. K. Acharya dated it very early. Tarapada Bhattacharyya argued it is a late “recension of recensions”, and Dagens agrees, treating any original as roughly contemporary with the Mayamata . Dagens also notes that Acharya's translation renders the sub-base and base as parts of a column.
        • 
          
      • The Manasara rules come from Acharya's 1933 translation  , read as excerpts returned by a fetch tool, not as full pages. Where the Mayamata gives the same rule, it is cited first.
        • 
          
      • One source counts 14 tiers at Thanjavur , against 13 elsewhere . Its height is 63.41 m in Balasubrahmanyam  and anything from about 61 m to 66 m in other sources consulted.
        • 
          
      • Tier heights step down evenly, as in the Mayamata . Hardy notes that those in the Samarangana follow no regular progression .
        • 
          
      • The dome and finial rest on texts alone. No source in this list describes their form in built temples.
        • 
          
      • The hall plan comes mostly from Gangaikondacholapuram, where the great hall's side walls, pillars and roof were rebuilt, and Balasubrahmanyam thinks the original was twice the present height . No measurements of Thanjavur's halls were found. Pichard's architectural study of Thanjavur (1995) probably has them and has not been read.
        • 
          
      • Balasubrahmanyam's ardhamandapa and Wikipedia's “antrala” are read as the same space, the hall with the north and south gateways  . That reading is about 75% certain. The Mayamata's covered passage in that position is only about two modules long , against a hall about 20 m long at Gangaikondacholapuram . The model follows the building.
        • 
          
      • No source read gives the height of the halls. Their pillar zone is set equal to the tower's first wall storey. Hall widths of pillars, walls and doors, the number of pillars outside the great hall, and the front hall's length are estimates.
        • 
          
      • Balasubrahmanyam puts Thanjavur's sanctum and ardhamandapa floors about 6.1 m above the courtyard . The measured sub-base and base add up to 5.0 m , and the model uses 5.0 m. The difference is unexplained.
        • 
          
      • The hall roofs are drawn flat, with no pavilions on them. The Mayamata allows either , and which one Thanjavur has was not checked. Its chapter on halls and pavilions (25, in volume 2) is known only from Dagens's summary in volume 1.
        • 
          
      • The halls leave out the guardian figures at the doors and the Nandi pavilion. The guardians are figure sculpture, which this page does not draw. Wikipedia dates the Nandi shrine to the Nayaka period , so it belongs to a later phase of the site.
        • 
          
      • The court, gateways, Nandi pavilion and court shrines are placed from OpenStreetMap outlines, measured for this project and checked against satellite imagery, to within about 1.5 to 4 m . That is not a published survey. The open court's inner edge is the least certain figure, at about 4 m.
        • 
          
      • The measured cloister, 241 by 121 m outside, matches the Archaeological Survey of India's 240.9 by 122 m , so that figure describes the outside of the walls. Balasubrahmanyam's 152.40 by 77.20 m  matches nothing on the site. His “almost a hundred metres” between the gateways also disagrees with the measured 71.9 m. It fits if counted from the later outermost gate, which is a guess.
        • 
          
      • No Chola wall round the outer gateway was found. The only outer wall is the later fort's, which runs through the gateway , so the gateway is drawn standing alone. ICOMOS's 270 by 140 m outer boundary  matches nothing mapped.
        • 
          
      • The real complex faces about 20° north of east. The model uses its own axis, so the light presets do not show the real sun angles.
        • 
          
      • The halls follow the mapped outline by default: their main east face is 67.2 m from the tower's centre, with a porch 10.5 m wide in front . Reading that porch as the mukhamandapa is an inference. How the length divides between the ardhamandapa and the great hall is estimated.
        • 
          
      • The map labels the small shrine north of the tower “Perumal temple”. It stands where the Chandesvara shrine should be, so it is drawn as that shrine, which is about 70% certain. The later shrines' names are also the map's own and unverified.
        • 
          
      • Wall height and thickness, pillar spacing and all building heights are estimates. The wall's height follows the Mayamata's rule tying it to the cloister . The cloister is drawn whole, but its upper storey has almost disappeared today .
        • 
          
      • The gateways' decoration comes mainly from photographs examined for this project , because the Mayamata's chapters on gateways are in its second volume, which was not available. Their heights are scaled from photographs, about ±15%. No height is published for the inner gateway; the outer one is drawn about 27 m, within reported heights of 25 to 30 m  . Pavilion counts on the tiers are only about 35 to 50% reliable, because later stucco figures hide them, and the short faces were not counted. The outer gateway's seven finials are about 60% likely. Where the granite stops and the brick begins is disputed  , and both are drawn the same colour. The 17th-century stucco figures on the tiers and the guardians' and panels' sculpture are not drawn.
        • 
          
      • ASI counts three side entrances to the court besides the gateway , and Balasubrahmanyam three entrances in all . Three side openings are drawn, at estimated positions. The cloister shrines' positions are invented.
        • 
          
      • Most sources date the present Nandi to the Nayak period  , and an earlier Chola Nandi is unverified.
        • 
          
      • The fort walls, the moat and the Maratha-period outer gate are left out. They are later than the temple  .
        • 
          
      • A parametric Karnata-Dravida model exists . No published Chola shape grammar was found, in a search that was not exhaustive.
        • 
          
      • Only Chola has been researched. Whether Pallava, Pandya, Vijayanagara and Nayak are distinct enough to be separate rule sets is unchecked.
