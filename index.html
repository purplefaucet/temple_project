    13
    Recorded counts: 13 at Thanjavur, 9 at Gangaikondacholapuram, 5 at Darasuram . Balasubrahmanyam gives thirteen storeys over the wall at Thanjavur but nine “including the ground tala” at Gangaikondacholapuram . The texts count from the ground up, to a maximum of 12 . Dagens reads a mention of sixteen storeys in the Mayamata as a later addition made for the large Chola temples . These ways of counting have not been reconciled here.

      • Texts and buildings are different kinds of evidence. Adam Hardy, who has tested texts by drawing them, says of text and measured building: “Often, they do correspond and often, they don't” . A rule taken from a text shows a design tradition. It does not show what Chola builders did.
        • 
          
      • No element has every dimension sourced. Switch “Colour by” to Dimensions to see which sizes rest on a cited ratio or measurement and which are estimates.
        • 
          
      • The measurements come from two temples only, both royal foundations. The base heights are Thanjavur's  and the wall height is Gangaikondacholapuram's . Mixing them into one tower is a choice made for this model.
        • 
          
      • How many pavilions a tier carries is set by how many parts fit its side, with each part about one storey tall. The Manasara gives every storey of a building the same row  . Thinning the rows on the narrower upper tiers is an inference, not a sourced rule.
        • 
          
      • The Manasara's date is contested. According to Wikipedia's summary of the scholarship, P. K. Acharya put it at about the 5th to 7th century, George Michell at the 7th to 8th, and Tarapada Bhattacharyya read the surviving complete text as a South Indian recension of about the 11th century or later, built up in layers. Those three works were not read directly for this page. If Bhattacharyya is right, the text as we have it is no earlier than the great Chola temples.
        • 
          
      • The Manasara rules come from Acharya's 1933 translation  , read as excerpts returned by a fetch tool, not as full pages. Only one complete manuscript is reported.
        • 
          
      • The Mayamata, which Hardy dates to before the end of the tenth century , is the closer text in time. Bruno Dagens's translation has not yet been read for this page. No Agama has been consulted.
        • 
          
      • Thanjavur is counted at 13 tiers by  and 14 by . Its height is given as 63.41 m by Balasubrahmanyam  and as anything from about 61 m to 66 m elsewhere.
        • 
          
      • Tier heights step down evenly from bottom to top. The Manasara's sequences are close to even steps  , while Hardy notes that those in the Samarangana follow no regular progression .
        • 
          
      • The dome and finial rest on the text alone. No source in this list describes their form in built temples. The one that did was an exam-prep summary and was removed.
        • 
          
      • A parametric Karnata-Dravida model exists . No published Chola shape grammar was found, in a search that was not exhaustive.
        • 
          
      • Only Chola has been researched. Whether Pallava, Pandya, Vijayanagara and Nayak are distinct enough to be separate rule sets is unchecked.
